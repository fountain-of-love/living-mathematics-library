# Prime Masks Are Finite Projections

## Context

Prime masks were being interpreted as finite operators before introducing any Hilbert-Polya-style spectral operator.

## Observation

Because a binary mask squares to itself, each prime mask is an idempotent multiplication operator and hence a projection on the corresponding finite residue function space.

## Mathematical expression

For a binary mask $B_p(n)$,

$$
B_p(n)^2=B_p(n).
$$

If $M_k$ is the combined finite mask formed from such binary exclusions, then

$$
M_k^2=M_k.
$$

## Example

On residues modulo $p$, a mask that is $0$ on the divisible class and $1$ elsewhere remains unchanged after pointwise multiplication by itself.

## Claim boundary

This establishes a finite projection contract for masks; it does not identify a limiting self-adjoint operator or prove a Hilbert-Polya mechanism.

## Interpretation

The grounded research question is which spectral operator, if any, is naturally generated by the nested family of finite prime projections.
