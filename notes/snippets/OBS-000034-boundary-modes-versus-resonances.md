# Separate Boundary Modes from Resonances

## Context

The draft needed vocabulary for separating modes that belong to the closed prime field from terms visible only in a broader analytic continuation.

## Observation

Connes's framework suggests a useful distinction between boundary-preserving spectral modes and boundary-drifting resonances.

## Mathematical expression

The proposed distinction is

$$
\text{boundary-preserving mode}\quad\text{versus}\quad\text{boundary-drifting resonance}.
$$

## Example

Connes interprets critical zeros spectrally while treating hypothetical noncritical zeros as resonance-like objects; see Connes, [Trace Formula in Noncommutative Geometry](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/9811068).

## Claim boundary

This is an interpretive vocabulary alignment, not a proof that noncritical zeros exist or that all critical zeros arise from the proposed prime-field closure.

## Interpretation

The distinction may help keep genuine closed-field modes separate from terms that appear through analytic continuation but fail the boundary-preservation contract.
