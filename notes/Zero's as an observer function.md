# Zeros as Observer Modes

The nontrivial zeros of the [[Mathematical fundamentals#Zeta function|Riemann zeta function]] are sometimes described as “observers” of the prime distribution. This is a useful picture if it is stated carefully.

Strictly speaking, a zero is not itself a function and does not perform a measurement. A zero

$$
\rho=\beta+i\gamma,
\qquad \zeta(\rho)=0,
$$

is a spectral parameter. The **observer function** is an explicit formula that combines the contributions of all zeros to reconstruct a weighted count of prime powers. In this sense:

- primes provide the arithmetic events;
- zeros provide the oscillatory observation modes;
- the explicit formula combines those modes into an observable counting signal.

## 1. The arithmetic signal

The natural signal is not initially the ordinary prime-counting function $\pi(x)$, but the Chebyshev function

$$
\psi(x)=\sum_{n\le x}\Lambda(n),
$$

where the von Mangoldt function is

$$
\Lambda(n)=
\begin{cases}
\log p,&n=p^m\text{ for some prime }p\text{ and }m\ge1,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
$$

Thus $\psi(x)$ records every prime-power event $p,p^2,p^3,\ldots$, weighted by $\log p$. It is well suited to analysis because

$$
-\frac{\zeta'(s)}{\zeta(s)}
=
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{n^s},
\qquad \Re(s)>1.
$$

This identity is the exact bridge from prime exclusions and prime powers to the analytic behavior of $\zeta(s)$.

## 2. The explicit observer formula

For $x>1$ away from prime-power discontinuities, one form of Riemann's explicit formula is

$$
\boxed{
\psi(x)
=x
-\sum_{\rho}\frac{x^\rho}{\rho}
-\log(2\pi)
-\frac12\log\left(1-x^{-2}\right)
}
$$

with the sum over nontrivial zeros interpreted symmetrically. At a discontinuity, the usual convention replaces $\psi(x)$ by the average of its left and right limits.

This formula acts as an observer of the prime distribution:

$$
\text{smooth average}
+
\text{zero modes}
+
\text{small correction terms}
=
\text{weighted prime-power count}.
$$

The leading term $x$ describes the global trend. The contribution

$$
O_\rho(x)=-\frac{x^\rho}{\rho}
$$

is the observation mode associated with the zero $\rho$. The collective correction

$$
\mathcal O(x)=-\sum_\rho\frac{x^\rho}{\rho}
$$

is therefore a natural mathematical realization of the observer function. It measures how the exact arithmetic signal departs from its smooth main term.

## 3. Why each zero behaves like a wave

Writing $\rho=\beta+i\gamma$ gives

$$
x^\rho=x^\beta e^{i\gamma\log x}.
$$

Hence every zero contributes:

- an amplitude scale $x^\beta$;
- a frequency $\gamma$ in the logarithmic coordinate $\log x$;
- a phase and weight supplied by $1/\rho$.

Because $\zeta(\overline{s})=\overline{\zeta(s)}$, non-real zeros occur in conjugate pairs. Combining $\rho$ and $\overline\rho$ produces a real oscillatory term:

$$
-\frac{x^\rho}{\rho}
-\frac{x^{\overline\rho}}{\overline\rho}
=
-2\operatorname{Re}\left(\frac{x^\rho}{\rho}\right).
$$

If the Riemann Hypothesis is true, then $\rho=\tfrac12+i\gamma$, and this becomes

$$
-2\sqrt{x}\,
\operatorname{Re}\left(
\frac{e^{i\gamma\log x}}{\tfrac12+i\gamma}
\right).
$$

After normalizing by $\sqrt{x}$, every individual zero mode has constant logarithmic amplitude. Its phase continues to oscillate as $\log x$ changes. This is the precise mathematical content behind the standing-wave analogy.

## 4. Recursive exclusions and spectral observation

The prime-exclusion recursion constructs the arithmetic field stage by stage. If $S_k(n)$ is the survivor field after the wave generated by $p_k$, then

$$
S_k(n)=S_{k-1}(n)W_k(n).
$$

Each new prime is discovered from the preceding survivor field and then introduces the next exclusion wave:

$$
S_k
\longrightarrow p_{k+1}
\longrightarrow W_{k+1}
\longrightarrow S_{k+1}.
$$

The zeta function encodes the completed multiplicative structure through Euler's product,

$$
\zeta(s)=\prod_p(1-p^{-s})^{-1},
\qquad \Re(s)>1.
$$

Taking the logarithmic derivative converts that product into the prime-power signal $\Lambda(n)$. The explicit formula then transforms this signal into a sum over zero modes. The two descriptions therefore concern the same arithmetic structure from different directions:

$$
\boxed{
\text{recursive prime exclusions}
\longrightarrow
\Lambda(n)
\longrightarrow
-\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(s)
\longrightarrow
\text{zeros}
\longrightarrow
\psi(x)
}
$$

The exclusions generate the distribution; the zero modes provide a global spectral description of the distribution and its fluctuations.

### The critical line as the balanced observation scale

The periodic masks already detect primality exactly. For $n>1$,

$$
\mathbf 1_{\mathbb P}(n)
=
\prod_{p\le\sqrt n}B_p(n),
$$

where $\mathbf 1_{\mathbb P}(n)=1$ precisely when $n$ is prime. This discrete observation does not depend on the Riemann Hypothesis or on the location of the zeta zeros.

The critical line enters when the same distribution is observed **spectrally**. Normalize the fluctuation in the Chebyshev function by its conjectural square-root scale:

$$
R(x)=\frac{\psi(x)-x}{\sqrt{x}}.
$$

A zero $\rho=\beta+i\gamma$ contributes to this normalized observer approximately

$$
-\frac{x^{\rho-1/2}}{\rho}
=
-\frac{x^{\beta-1/2}}{\rho}
e^{i\gamma\log x}.
$$

Its behavior is therefore determined by its horizontal position:

$$
\begin{array}{ccl}
\beta=\tfrac12
&\Longrightarrow&
\text{constant amplitude on the }\sqrt{x}\text{ scale},\\
\beta>\tfrac12
&\Longrightarrow&
\text{growing amplitude},\\
\beta<\tfrac12
&\Longrightarrow&
\text{decaying amplitude}.
\end{array}
$$

Thus the critical line is naturally interpreted as an **equilibrium observation scale**. If the Riemann Hypothesis is true, every nontrivial zero observes the recursively generated prime distribution as a balanced oscillation in $\log x$.

If an off-line zero existed, however, the explicit formula would still reconstruct the prime distribution. We would not lose sight of the primes through the sieve; instead, the corresponding spectral mode would display power-law drift relative to the $\sqrt{x}$ normalization. The mathematically careful statement is therefore:

> The bit masks reveal the primes arithmetically. The zeros observe their global fluctuations spectrally. The Riemann Hypothesis says that every nontrivial observation mode is balanced on the critical line.

## 5. Symmetry of the observation modes

It is best to express the functional equation using the completed zeta function

$$
\xi(s)
=
\frac12s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}
\Gamma\left(\frac{s}{2}\right)\zeta(s),
$$

which satisfies

$$
\xi(s)=\xi(1-s).
$$

Together with complex conjugation, this implies that an off-critical-line zero generally belongs to the symmetric family

$$
\rho,
\quad \overline\rho,
\quad 1-\rho,
\quad 1-\overline\rho.
$$

Here $\rho$ denotes a hypothetical **nontrivial** zero. This quartet is distinct from the trivial zeros $-2,-4,-6,\ldots$ of $\zeta(s)$, which are canceled by poles of the gamma factor when $\xi(s)$ is formed.

The line

$$
\operatorname{Re}(s)=\frac12
$$

is the fixed line of the reflection $s\mapsto1-s$. Zeros on this line pair as

$$
\frac12+i\gamma
\quad\longleftrightarrow\quad
\frac12-i\gamma.
$$

This symmetry explains why the critical line is distinguished. It does **not**, by itself, prove that every zero lies there: symmetric quartets away from the line are also compatible with the functional equation.

## 6. Observer as physical-state alignment

Framing the zeros as observation modes or measurements of physical-state alignment leads naturally to the **Hilbert–Pólya program** and to quantum-chaotic models of the zeros.

In quantum mechanics, the stationary states of a system satisfy an eigenvalue equation

$$
H\phi_E=E\phi_E.
$$

The Hilbert–Pólya proposal is that there exists a suitable self-adjoint operator $H$ whose complete spectrum corresponds to the nontrivial zeta zeros through

$$
\boxed{\rho=\frac12+iE}.
$$

This would turn the imaginary ordinates of the zeros into measurable energy levels. It is a conditional spectral model: the required operator and the exact correspondence have not yet been constructed and proved.

### 6.1 Self-adjointness and real measurements

A self-adjoint operator has real eigenvalues:

$$
H=H^*
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
E\in\mathbb R.
$$

Under the Hilbert–Pólya correspondence, a general zero $\rho=\beta+i\gamma$ would require the spectral value

$$
E=\frac{\rho-\tfrac12}{i}
=
\gamma-i\left(\beta-\frac12\right).
$$

Therefore,

$$
E\in\mathbb R
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\beta=\frac12.
$$

This is the rigorous core of the Hermitian intuition. If one could prove that **every** nontrivial zero arises from the spectrum of this self-adjoint $H$, the reality of its spectrum would force every zero onto the critical line.

An off-line zero would not literally make a physical system gain or lose energy. It would mean that the proposed self-adjoint operator does not represent all the zeros, or that one of the assumptions used to construct the spectral model fails. Non-self-adjoint operators can also describe open or dissipative systems, so non-Hermiticity is not by itself a logical contradiction.

### 6.2 Functional reflection and the time-reversal analogy

Center the completed zeta function at the critical line by writing

$$
s=\frac12+z.
$$

Its functional equation becomes

$$
\xi\left(\frac12+z\right)
=
\xi\left(\frac12-z\right).
$$

On the critical line, $z=iE$, so reflection sends

$$
E\longmapsto-E.
$$

This resembles the pairing of forward and reversed spectral modes and motivates a time-reversal metaphor. The fixed line $\operatorname{Re}(s)=1/2$ is indeed the balance axis of the functional equation.

The analogy has a precise limit: functional reflection is not automatically a physical time-reversal operator. If $z$ has a nonzero real part, the two points $z$ and $-z$ remain perfectly reflection-symmetric. Consequently, hypothetical off-line quartets do **not** break the functional equation. Additional spectral structure—such as a proved self-adjoint realization—is needed to exclude them.

There is also an important quantum-chaos distinction: the local statistics of high zeta zeros are modeled by the Gaussian unitary ensemble, the symmetry class normally associated with chaotic systems **without** ordinary time-reversal invariance. The reflection $s\mapsto1-s$ should therefore not be identified directly with physical $T$-invariance.

### 6.3 Why the physical observer perspective is useful

A careful physical dictionary is:

1. **Prime powers** act like periodic-orbit or scattering data, entering through $\Lambda(n)$ and the logarithms of primes.
2. **The zeta function** packages this arithmetic information into an analytic object analogous to a spectral determinant or trace-generating function.
3. **The zeros** behave like spectral levels or resonances whose modes reconstruct prime-counting fluctuations through the explicit formula.

Berry and Keating developed the analogy between zero statistics and the spectrum of an unknown Hermitian quantum-chaotic system. Connes developed a related interpretation in which critical zeros appear as an absorption spectrum and hypothetical noncritical zeros as resonances. These are substantial mathematical programs, but neither currently proves that every zero lies on the critical line.

Standing-wave language is therefore appropriate as a model: on the critical line, each normalized zero mode has balanced amplitude and real spectral parameter $E$. The statement that an observer **cannot** detect an off-line zero becomes a theorem only after the existence, self-adjointness, and completeness of the proposed observer operator have all been established.

## 7. What “observer” should mean

The observer language is strongest when used at three levels:

1. **Exact arithmetic level:** $\psi(x)$ observes accumulated prime-power events.
2. **Spectral level:** each zero supplies an oscillatory mode $O_\rho(x)$.
3. **Reconstruction level:** the explicit formula superposes all modes to recover the arithmetic signal around its smooth trend.

The physically inspired language of resonances, energy levels, and wave interference can guide intuition. It should not be mistaken for a proof that the zeros lie on the critical line. The mathematically established statement is that the zeros encode fluctuations in prime counting through explicit formulas; the spectral-operator explanation for why all their real parts should equal $1/2$ remains conjectural.
